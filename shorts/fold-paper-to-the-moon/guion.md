SOURCES:
- Paper thickness assumed 0.1 mm (on screen: "ASSUMES 0.1 MM PAPER") — U.S. National Nanotechnology
  Initiative figure of ~100,000 nm per sheet, via https://scaleofuniverse.com/universe/thickness-of-paper ;
  500 sheets of 20 lb paper measured at 51.5 mm = 0.103 mm/sheet (https://hypertextbook.com/facts/2001/JuliaSherlis.shtml).
  Copy paper ranges ~0.07–0.10 mm (https://www.bachelorprint.com/printing-guide/printer-paper/paper-weight/paper-thickness).
  ⚠ With 0.08 mm paper, 42 folds = ~352,000 km (short of the Moon) and 43 are needed; at 0.1 mm the
  Moon threshold is 41.8 folds. Hence the on-screen assumption line.
- Thickness after n folds = 0.1 mm × 2^n (each fold doubles the layers). Computed:
  14 → 1.64 m (5 ft 4 in) · 23 → 839 m (2,752 ft) · 30 → 107 km (67 mi) · 32 → 429 km (267 mi) ·
  41 → 220,000 km · 42 → 440,000 km (273,000 mi) · 51 → 225 million km (140 million mi) ·
  102 → 53.6 billion light-years · 103 → 107 billion light-years.
- Moon's average distance 384,400 km (238,855 mi) — NASA, https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/
  (42 folds = 440,000 km > 384,400 km; it also beats the apogee, ~405,500 km)
- "About your height": average U.S. adult man 5 ft 9.2 in, woman 5 ft 3.7 in (CDC NCHS / NHANES,
  https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/fastats/body-measurements.htm); 14 folds = 5 ft 4 in sits between them
- World's tallest building: Burj Khalifa, 828 m (2,717 ft) — CTBUH, https://www.skyscrapercenter.com/building/burj-khalifa/3
  (23 folds = 839 m: taller). Drawn as a GENERIC supertall tower, not the Burj (no recognizable landmarks, §3.9).
- Edge of space: Kármán line, 100 km (62 mi), FAI definition — https://www.space.com/karman-line-where-does-space-begin
  (30 folds = 107 km: above it). Note: the US Air Force/NASA astronaut-wings line is 80 km; 30 folds clears both.
- ISS altitude ~400 km (~250 mi), varies with reboosts — https://www.space.com/16748-international-space-station.html
  (32 folds = 429 km: above it)
- Sun's average distance 149.6 million km (93 million mi) — NASA NSSDCA Sun Fact Sheet,
  https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/sunfact.html (51 folds = 225 million km: past it;
  50 folds = 113 million km, short)
- Observable universe ~93 billion light-years across — Britannica, https://www.britannica.com/topic/observable-universe
  (published estimates 92–94; NASA rounds to ~92). 103 folds = 107 billion ly beats every estimate;
  102 folds (53.6) does not.
- Record: 13 folds, students and teacher James Tanton of St. Mark's School, MIT's Infinite Corridor,
  Dec 4, 2011, ~54,000 ft (10 miles, ~16 km) of single-ply toilet paper — NPR, Dec 5, 2011,
  https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/12/05/143150449/record-folders-54-000-feet-of-paper-13-folds-one-new-standard
  (NPR calls it an unofficial record and "the equivalent of 13 folds"; MIT's alumni site says 53,000 ft;
  rolls were taped together: New Scientist via https://boingboing.net/2012/01/12/new-record-toilet-paper-folde.html ;
  the first 64 sheets were layered rather than folded: https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/62865/how-many-times-can-you-fold-piece-paper).
  ⚠ DEBATED: Britney Gallivan holds the Guinness record, 12 folds of ONE continuous sheet (4,000 ft
  of toilet paper, Pomona, CA, Jan 27, 2002 — https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/494571-most-times-to-fold-a-piece-of-paper)
  and disputes the 13 because the paper was taped. That is why the VO says "Humans have only managed
  13" (true: 13 folds were achieved) and does NOT say "the world record is 13". The ~7 hours figure
  belongs to Gallivan's attempt (CSIRO), not St. Mark's: not used.
- "Each fold needs way more paper": the length required grows roughly with the square of the number
  of layers (Gallivan's folding-limit formula) — Science News, https://www.sciencenews.org/article/folding-paper-half-twelve-times
- 10 miles ≈ 176 football fields: 52,800 ft ÷ 300 ft (100 yards goal line to goal line, NFL rulebook).
