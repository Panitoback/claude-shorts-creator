SOURCES:
- Speed: New Horizons left Earth at ~36,000 mph (16.26 km/s), the fastest departure of any spacecraft — NASA, "15 Years Ago: New Horizons Launched to Pluto and Beyond" (https://www.nasa.gov/history/15-years-ago-new-horizons-launched-to-pluto-and-beyond/); NASA launch release "NASA's Pluto Mission Launched Toward New Horizons" (https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-pluto-mission-launched-toward-new-horizons/); exact 16.26 km/s: Scientific American, "The Fastest Spacecraft Ever?" (Earth-relative launch speed)
- Minimum distance from Earth (NASA NSSDCA planetary fact sheets, https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/<planet>fact.html):
  Venus 38.2 · Mars 54.6 · Mercury 77.3 · Jupiter 588.5 · Saturn 1,205.5 · Uranus 2,580.6 · Neptune 4,319.0 (million km)
- Times = distance ÷ 16.26 km/s (1,404,864 km per day):
  Venus 27.2 d → "27 days" · Mars 38.9 d → "39 days" · Mercury 55.0 d → "55 days" · Jupiter 419 d = 13.8 mo → "14 months" · Saturn 858 d = 2.35 yr → "2.3 years" · Uranus 1,837 d = 5.03 yr → "5 years" · Neptune 3,074 d = 8.42 yr → "8.4 years"
- Closing line: the real New Horizons reached Jupiter in 13 months (launched 19 Jan 2006, closest approach 28 Feb 2007) — NASA, "NASA Spacecraft Sees Changes in Jupiter System"
- Caveats (covered by the RULE LINE): real trajectories curve around the Sun, the Sun's gravity slows an outbound craft, and the planets are never all at their closest at the same time
